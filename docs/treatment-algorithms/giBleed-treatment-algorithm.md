# Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding — Educational Treatment Pathway

This is an educational reference, not validated clinical decision support. The simulator provides only scripted vital signs and fixed intervention deltas; it does not model hemoglobin, bleeding source, cirrhosis, endoscopy, or hemostasis.

## Case snapshot

The scripted case starts at NIBP 96/60, HR 112, SpO₂ 96%, RR 20, temperature 36.9 °C, and glucose 145. Untreated values worsen over 60 minutes. Vitals alone cannot replace hemoglobin testing, Glasgow-Blatchford assessment, or evaluation for active hemorrhage.

## Educational approach

- Resuscitate and assess the source. Do not wait for a hemoglobin result or a fixed number of transfused units before activating an emergency hemorrhage pathway when exsanguinating bleeding is suspected. Restrictive hemoglobin thresholds apply to stable patients, not uncontrolled exsanguination. ESGE suggests a higher threshold (hemoglobin ≥10 g/dL) in stable patients with cardiovascular disease, based on low-quality evidence; do not universalize a 7–9 g/dL target.
- Separate pre-endoscopy PPI use, which may be considered, from high-dose PPI therapy for 72 hours after endoscopic hemostasis of a high-risk ulcer.
- In unstable patients, perform urgent endoscopy after resuscitation. CTA is not an automatic replacement. For recurrent bleeding, repeat therapeutic endoscopy, then consider embolization and surgery if needed.
- Test for *H. pylori*, treat when present, and confirm eradication.
- For suspected variceal bleeding, provide specialist-directed vasoactive therapy, antibiotic prophylaxis, and endoscopic treatment. Esophageal banding is not a universal gastric-varix treatment. Consider pre-emptive TIPS for selected high-risk patients and rescue TIPS when indicated; tamponade or a stent is a monitored bridge, not definitive therapy.
- Endoscopy timing policy remains to be selected for this educational pathway: ESGE recommends within 12 hours after resuscitation and vasoactive therapy up to 5 days for suspected variceal hemorrhage; ASGE 2026 recommends within 24 hours with individualized timing and vasoactive therapy for 2–3 days. The simulator does not represent either standard.
- Treat symptomatic bradycardia only when it causes cardiopulmonary compromise; HR is often <50 in this context. Assess ABCs, pulse, ECG, reversible causes, and arrest; persistent symptoms after atropine require escalation under current ACLS/local protocol. These actions are not modeled.

## Model boundary and unresolved policy

The simulator has no hemoglobin, GBS, endoscopy, variceal phenotype, or bleed-control state. Its “Blood transfusion (1 unit)” is a fixed vital-sign delta, not transfusion guidance. A single named variceal timing standard must be selected before this educational pathway can be presented as one operational protocol.
